Questions in, charts out

A multi-agent system that analyses public datasets from plain-language questions: self-service civic analytics for everyone.

  • Turns natural-language questions into automated data analysis and visualisations, using GPT-4.1, Python and Streamlit.

MSc thesis · LUISS Guido Carli · 2026

When passengers show up

My MSc thesis forecasts how many passengers reach the check-in desks at Rome Fiumicino, quarter hour by quarter hour, so the airport can staff them ahead.

Build a morning at the desks

Switch flights on and off and set how many passengers use a desk instead of checking in online. Each flight spreads its passengers over the hours before departure; the curve is their sum.

The model runs in your browser; turn on JavaScript to draw the curve.

Busiest quarter hour – with – passengers at the desks, so – desks open.

Illustrative curves that show the idea, not Fiumicino data. Desks assume two minutes per passenger.

How it works

Check-ins are counted in bins from 5 to 30 minutes and forecast from one hour to one week ahead. The features are leakage-free: a show-up kernel, the typical arrival pattern before a departure, is convolved with the flight schedule.

A multi-horizon LightGBM model on those features cut error by 29–33% against the seasonal baseline, statistically significant on every setup.

Benchmarked against

  • XGBoost and CatBoost
  • TiDE and Temporal Fusion Transformer, trained on AWS SageMaker GPUs
  • TimesFM 3.0, a time-series foundation model fine-tuned with LoRA

Advisor Giuseppe F. Italiano, co-advisor Vincenzo Lomonaco. LUISS Guido Carli, 2026.
